Statutory definition
A person is guilty of theft if he dishonestly appropriates property belonging to another with the intention of permanently depriving the other of it (Theft Act 1968 s.1(1))
Five elements to prove

  2. Property (tangible personal property)

  5. Intention to permanently deprive

Alternative Charge

Charge
Handling stolen goods contrary to s.22 Theft Act 1968
Status
Alternative charge (only pursued if primary charge cannot be proven)
Statutory definition
A person is guilty of handling stolen goods if, knowing or believing them to be stolen goods, he dishonestly receives the goods, or dishonestly undertakes or assists in their retention, removal, disposal or realization by another person (Theft Act 1968 s.22(1))
Key elements
  • The goods must be stolen goods (proven by primary charge)
  • Knowing or believing them to be stolen
  • Dishonestly receives or assists in their retention/removal
Rationale for alternative
If the court is not satisfied beyond reasonable doubt that defendant appropriated with dishonest intent to permanently deprive, they may still find her guilty of handling if she dishonestly received goods she knew or believed to be stolen.
