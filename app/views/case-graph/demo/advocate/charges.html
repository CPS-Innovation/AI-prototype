Theft contrary to s.1 Theft Act 1968
Status
Proposed
Defendants
Tabitha Quibble, Percival Snodgrass
Statutory definition
A person is guilty of theft if he dishonestly appropriates property belonging to another with the intention of permanently depriving the other of it (Theft Act 1968 s.1(1))
AI generated evidence assessment
Most essential evidence for a shoplifting case is present, but there is contradictory evidence regarding dishonesty and intention to permanently deprive. CCTV footage, recovered items, and witness testimony support the charge, but witness and defendant statements introduce doubt about intent and possible mistake.
Points to prove
  1. Appropriates property (assumes control) Very strong
    CCTV footage shows defendant taking items from shelf and concealing them.
    Evidence which appears to support
    • CCTV footage (Exihibit 01, 14:20-14:25) - Quibble selects items and places directly in personal bag (assumes control)
    • CCTV footage (Exhibit 01, 14:22-14:26) - Snodgrass assists Quibble, stands lookout, handles items before placing in bag
    • Security guard statement (Exhibit 08) - Witnessed defendant handling and concealing items
    • Recovered items (Exhibit 04) - All items found in defendants' possession
  2. Property (tangible personal property) Very strong
    Store ownership documentation and witness statement from manager confirm property belonged to Selfridges at time of theft.
    Evidence which appears to support
    • Recovered items (Exhibit 04) - 5 items of clothing, tangible personal property
    • Store inventory (Exhibit 11) - Items listed with product codes and descriptions
    • Store records (Exhibit 12) - Items capable of possession and use
  3. Belonging to another Very strong
    Defendant concealed items and attempted to leave without payment, indicating dishonest intent.
    Evidence which appears to support
    • Store records (Exhibit 12) - Items owned by Selfridges at time of alleged theft
    • Recovered items (Exhibit 04) - All items bearing Selfridges branding and security tags at time of removal
    • Store records (Exhibit 12) - Selfridges's property rights in inventory

    Defendant concealed items and attempted to leave without payment, but there is evidence suggesting possible confusion or mistake.
    Evidence which appears to support

    Evidence which appears to contradict

    • Defendant interview (Exhibit 10) - Defendant claimed to have forgotten to pay and offered to return items when stopped
  5. Intention to permanently deprive Moderate
    Attempt to leave store with concealed items and no payment made suggests intent to permanently deprive, but there is evidence of possible mistake or lack of intent.
    Evidence supporting
    • CCTV footage (Exihibit 01, 14:24-14:25) - Defendant walked directly toward exit without attempting to pay or return items
    • Security guard statement (Exhibit 08) - When confronted by security, defendant made no attempt to explain mistake or claim misunderstanding
    • Criminal history (Exhibit 02) - Snodgrass has 2 previous shoplifting convictions (March 2024, September 2024), demonstrating pattern of conduct
    • Defendant statement (Exhibit 03) - Quibble made no comment when interviewed (standard caution) - s.34 CJPA 1994 inference available
    • Defendant statement (Exhibit 03) - Snodgrass made no comment when interviewed (standard caution) - s.34 CJPA 1994 inference available
    Evidence against

    • Defendant interview (Exhibit 10) - Defendant claimed to have forgotten to pay and offered to return items when stopped
