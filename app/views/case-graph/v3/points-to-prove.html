Charge 2: Assault occasioning actual bodily harm s.47 Offences Against the Person Act 1861

Applies to David Morgan only. Statutory Definition: A person is guilty if they commit common assault and thereby cause actual bodily harm to another - OAPA 1861 s.47

1. Applied force (common assault)

✓ PROVEN

Evidence Strength: STRONG

Evidence that supports this point
  • CCTV (14:28-14:30) - Morgan intentionally pushes Patterson with both hands as she approaches
  • Witness Statement (Patterson) - Direct account of being pushed by Morgan
  • Witness Statement (Store Assistant) - Saw Morgan make deliberate pushing motion toward guard

Source: CCTV footage, witness statements (MG11)

2. Intentional or reckless

✓ PROVEN

Evidence Strength: STRONG

Evidence that supports this point
  • CCTV (14:28) - Morgan's movement is deliberate and purposeful (not accidental contact)
  • Timing Analysis - Push occurs immediately when Patterson approaches to intercede
  • Witness Account - No suggestion this was accidental; Patterson describes deliberate action

Source: CCTV footage, witness testimony

3. Actual bodily harm (ABH) occasioned

✓ PROVEN

Evidence Strength: STRONG

Evidence that supports this point
  • Medical Report (Dr S. Williams) - Patterson suffered bruising to right shoulder and upper arm, confirmed ABH threshold
  • A&E Attendance Record - Attended hospital same day, documented injuries consistent with blunt force trauma
  • GP Follow-up Notes - Required follow-up appointments; injury caused pain and restricted arm movement
  • Photographic Evidence - Bruising photographed and documented

Source: Medical evidence (Dr Williams), A&E records, photographic evidence

4. Causal link: force caused ABH

✓ PROVEN

Evidence Strength: STRONG

Evidence that supports this point
  • Timeline - Injuries sustained immediately after Morgan's push (14:28)
  • Medical Evidence - Injuries consistent with push/fall mechanism; no other cause apparent
  • Witness Account - Patterson fell directly as result of push; injuries from contact and fall
  • Scene Examination - Hard floor and nearby displays; injuries consistent with fall pattern

Source: Medical evidence, witness testimony, scene documentation
